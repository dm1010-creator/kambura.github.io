Serve it right

The brew method decides what leaves the cup — and what stays in it

The same bean, brewed four ways, delivers four different bodies of compounds. Match the method to what you actually want out of your coffee, including your lipid profile.

Paper filter (V60 / Chemex)

Clarity above all. Paper traps almost all the oil-bound diterpenes, leaving single-origin character front and centre.

  • Burr grinder, medium grind
  • Gooseneck kettle, 91–94°C
  • Ratio 1:15–1:18
Near-zero cafestol/kahweol — the choice for anyone managing LDL cholesterol.

French press / plunger

Metal mesh lets oils and fines through for full body and mouthfeel — richer, heavier, more textured than any paper method.

  • Coarse grind
  • 4-minute steep
  • Ratio 1:12–1:15
Highest diterpene retention — enjoy it, but not the cup to default to daily if LDL is a concern.

Espresso

90–95°C, ~9 bar, 25–30 seconds. Forced emulsification concentrates flavour and crema — moderate oil carryover, maximum intensity.

  • Fine grind, PID-controlled boiler
  • 18–20g dose, 1:2 yield
Moderate diterpene levels — a middle ground between filter and plunger.

Cold brew

12–18 hour room-temp or fridge steep. Lower extraction, naturally sweeter, gentler on delicate esters — a good afternoon option.

  • Coarse grind, 1:8 ratio
  • Steep, then filter
Diterpene levels vary by filtration — finish through paper if cholesterol is a factor.
Why our dripper looks the way it does

We built our house dripper around a peer-reviewed filter-cup study

Researchers compared a standard commercial cone against two purpose-built ceramic drippers, holding beans, grind, dose and pour identical. Only the groove geometry changed.

  1. What changed

    The winning design used eight alternating long-and-short grooves that hold the paper filter tight against the ceramic wall, instead of the loose spiral ridge found in a standard commercial cone.

  2. Why it mattered

    A snug paper seal lets air vent evenly and water spread in a stable front — no channelling, no dry pockets of grounds, no clogged fines at the base.

  3. What it produced

    Higher caffeine, chlorogenic acid, trigonelline, total phenolics, total flavonoids and antioxidant activity (DPPH & ABTS) than the commercial control — plus more consistent flow, even across different people pouring.

Built for your body

The right cup depends on more than taste

Coffee's cardiometabolic upside — lower cardiovascular, type 2 diabetes and neurodegenerative disease risk at moderate intake — holds up across age, sex and geography. But a few personal variables still change what “moderate” and “right” mean for you.

Metabolism

CYP1A2 genetics set how fast you clear caffeine. Fast metabolisers tolerate more without cardiovascular strain; slow metabolisers may see blood pressure or LDL effects at the same dose.

Timing

The cortisol window. Waiting 60–90 minutes after waking, once natural cortisol has peaked and begun to fall, gets more net alertness out of the same cup.

Sleep

A 2pm cup is still active at 9pm for most people. If sleep is sensitive, switch to cascara or a low-dose filter brew after early afternoon.

Pregnancy

Guidance holds at under 200 mg/day of caffeine, with many clinicians recommending decaf through the first trimester.
