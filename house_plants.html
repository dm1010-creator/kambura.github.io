Everyday questions

Quick answers

Can I eat a snake plant or a spider plant?

No. The snake plant contains saponins and can cause nausea, vomiting and mouth irritation if eaten. The spider plant is listed as non-toxic to people and pets, but it is an ornamental, not a food, so do not eat it. Non-toxic does not mean edible.

How much water does each plant need?

It depends on pot size, so use the table above as a starting point: roughly 100 to 200 ml for a small snake plant pot and 150 to 250 ml for a small spider plant pot. Push a finger 2 to 3 cm into the soil first. If it is still damp, wait.

How long does it take to grow a new plant?

Spider plant plantlets usually root in 2 to 4 weeks once potted or put in water. Snake plant leaf cuttings take about 6 to 8 weeks to root and longer to grow shoots, so dividing a crowded pot is the fast route. Yellow-edged snake plants usually turn plain green when grown from leaf cuttings, so divide them instead to keep the stripes.

What kind of pot and soil?

Any pot with a drainage hole. Use loose, free-draining soil; mixing in some coarse sand helps. Standing water at the bottom is what rots roots.

Why are the leaves turning yellow, soft or brown?

Yellow leaves or a mushy snake plant base almost always mean too much water, so stop watering and let it dry. Brown tips on a spider plant usually mean uneven watering or very dry soil.

When should I repot, and do I need fertiliser?

Repot every one to two years, or when roots fill the pot. Snake plants like to be snug. A light feed once a month while the plant is actively growing is plenty, and not essential.

Can they live outside?

Yes, in bright shade. Avoid harsh midday sun, which scorches leaves, and heavy rain that leaves the pot waterlogged.

Are they safe for cats, dogs and children?

The ASPCA lists the snake plant as mildly toxic to cats and dogs if chewed, so keep it out of reach. It lists the spider plant as non-toxic. Some websites claim the spider plant irritates cats, so if your pet chews plants, keep both out of reach to be safe.

Do they clean the air or help me sleep?

Not in any way you would notice. Sealed-box tests show plants absorb some airborne chemicals, but a 2019 review of 196 results found the effect in real rooms is far too small to matter; an open window does much more. Snake plants take in carbon dioxide at night, but we know of no good evidence that one plant changes the oxygen in a bedroom. What small studies do support is that tending plants can feel calming.

Do they flower?

Spider plants can produce small white flowers, followed by baby plantlets. Indoor snake plants rarely flower.
