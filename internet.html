Digital infrastructure · Research note

No single actor governs the Internet.

Digital governance is achieved through the collaboration of multistakeholder experts acting through polycentric communities, institutions and platforms. The issues themselves stratify into three layers — infrastructure, logical, and economic-societal — each with its own technical communities and key actors.

Layer 1 · Physical substrate

The Infrastructure Layer

The physical networks that carry packets. Roughly 90 % of the Internet backbone (IP networks) is privately owned by global companies. Governance here is largely technical and operator-driven.

Ownership reality

Major backbone operators include Level 3, Telefónica International Carrier, CenturyLink, Vodafone, Verizon, Sprint, AT&T and regional peers. The physical layer is overwhelmingly private.

Layer 2 · Unique identifiers & protocols

The Logical Layer

Layered on top of the physical substrate, the logical infrastructure delivers the One Internet through unique identifiers — Names, Numbers and Protocol Parameters. ICANN coordinates administration of this layer in partnership with other technical communities to ensure security, stability, resiliency and integrity.

Names · Numbers · Protocols · Registries

Logical
Root Zone + Names + Numbers + Internet Protocols + Identifiers / Public Registries

Root Services

12 organisations from 4 countries administer 13 root-server letters that provide top-level DNS services via hundreds of machines in dozens of countries. ~500 anycast copies worldwide.

Domain Names

~300 Country-Code Top-Level Domains (ccTLDs). ~600+ Generic TLDs (gTLDs). 1,300+ Domain Name Registrars (GoDaddy, Network Solutions, Register…).

IP Addresses

IPv4: more than 4 billion addresses. IPv6: 340 undecillion addresses. 5 Regional Internet Registries (RIRs) coordinate policy related to Internet address resources.

Protocol Parameters

The commands and identifiers used inside protocols — the structured communications for web, email, etc. Defined by the IETF in coordination with other standards bodies (W3C, IEEE…).

Technical operations community. The technical operating community is made up of multiple independent actors bound by common principles and mutual commitments that ensure the security and stability of the Internet infrastructure. Each actor’s community develops policies and standards in an open, inclusive and consensus-based approach.

Layer 3 · Human activity on the network

The Economic & Societal Layer

Governing bodies from local, national, regional and international spheres engage with citizens and other bodies to develop and apply laws, policies and regulations. The transnational nature of the Internet must be synchronized with the established international system of governance and laws.

Content · Commerce · Rights · Culture

Economic & Societal

Laws, Policies & Regulations

Governing bodies develop and apply laws, policies and regulations. The transnational character of the Internet requires coordination with the established international legal system.

Industry & Trade

Manufacturing, retail, supply chain, logistics, healthcare, finance and related sectors. Global platforms (Amazon, eBay, Alibaba, Rakuten, Flipkart…) operate across jurisdictions.

News & Information

Newspapers, broadcast, personal and professional blogs, social media. The layer where public discourse and information flows are shaped.

Users

Over 3 billion users worldwide. Most users connect through mobile phones. Regional distribution continues to shift toward Asia, Africa and Latin America.

Civic & Human Rights

Privacy, identity, access to content, freedom of expression, cybersecurity, consumer protection, cultural diversity and related rights.

Social Media & Security

Sharing of photos, videos, ideas and information. Cybersecurity, cyber warfare, cyber espionage and related threats sit at this layer alongside the applications that carry them.

Education & Applications

Online universities, research, tutorials, classroom engagement. World Wide Web, email, cloud, VoIP, mobile apps, entertainment platforms.

Developmental note

Institutions inherit order; they do not create it

The logical layer of the Internet works because technical communities already recognised legitimate authority — root operators, RIR policy processes, IETF working groups — long before formal intergovernmental structures attempted to oversee them. The same developmental sequence holds for any durable civic or commercial system: presence and reciprocity precede contracts; recognised authority precedes statute. The three layers of digital governance are therefore not merely an organisational chart. They are a living demonstration that order is formed by communities capable of recognising legitimate technical and civic authority, and only afterwards formalised by institutions.

This page exists so that the same principle can be read clearly when the coffee research site itself is deployed on GitHub Pages — one more node in the logical and infrastructure layers, governed by the same multistakeholder practices that keep the wider Internet stable.
